## Motivation

The Higgs boson's couplings to third-generation fermions are now measured
directly. Its couplings to the **first and second generation quarks** are not,
and they are hard to reach: the corresponding decay widths are tiny, and the
inclusive light-quark final states are hopelessly swamped by QCD background at a
hadron collider.

Exclusive decays to a light vector meson plus a photon offer a way in. The final
state carries a clean, fully reconstructible signature, and the decay amplitude
interferes with an indirect contribution in which the photon is radiated and the
meson is produced through the Higgs coupling to the quark pair. That interference
is what makes the channel sensitive to the light-quark Yukawa couplings rather
than merely to the total width.

The branching fractions are extremely small in the Standard Model, so a
measurement is not the near-term goal. The near-term goal is a limit — and any
significant excess over the Standard Model expectation would be direct evidence
of modified light-quark couplings.

## Analysis

The experimental problem is a narrow resonant signal on a large, smooth
continuum background. That shapes the work:

- reconstructing the meson candidate from its charged decay products and pairing
  it with a high-transverse-momentum photon;
- suppressing continuum backgrounds where a jet fakes the meson candidate, or a
  jet fragment fakes the photon;
- building a background model that is flexible enough to describe the continuum
  without absorbing a genuine signal, and quantifying the bias if it does;
- setting limits on the branching fraction, and translating those into
  constraints on the light-quark coupling modifiers.
