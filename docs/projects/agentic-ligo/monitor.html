Why honest uncertainties win

If the real error is Gaussian with width $\sigma_\mathrm{true}$, the expected charge per world is $\ln(\sigma/\sigma_\mathrm{ref}) + \big((\sigma_\mathrm{true}/\sigma)^2 - 1\big)/2$, lowest at $\sigma = \sigma_\mathrm{true}$. With the shared-bias term over $K$ worlds, the pull part grows by $(1 + 1/K)$ and the best choice moves to $\sigma_\mathrm{true}\sqrt{1 + 1/K}$.

A bias shared by every world

The answers from all $K$ worlds are combined like independent measurements; $B = (z^{\ast 2} - 1)/(2K)$ is added once to the quantity's mean charge. A random error averages out in $z^\ast$; a repeated bias does not. Every world here quotes the reference's uncertainty.
