Note added 14 October 2026: the SNR population on this page

The power-law populations on this page draw each injected signal's SNR with probability density ∝ SNR⁻³ ("index −3"; ∝ SNR⁻² where "index −2" is stated). Sources spread uniformly in space give a steeper density, ∝ SNR⁻⁴, so that the fraction louder than a given SNR falls as SNR⁻³ (Schutz 2011, arXiv:1102.5421; derivation). These populations therefore hold relatively more loud events than real detections: from SNR 4, 75.1 to 75.5% of events are below SNR 8 (for a maximum SNR of 100 or 50), against 87.5% for sources uniform in space.

Results within an SNR range, and comparisons between models tested on the same population, are not affected. Averages over a whole population (the "all events" rows and overall validation numbers) are higher than they would be for sources uniform in space, for any measure that improves with SNR.
